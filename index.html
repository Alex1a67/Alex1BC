Setup & Tutorial Guide

Step-by-step instructions to get you tracking in under 2 minutes. No complicated calibration required.

STEP 01 // COMPUTER BLENDER 4.2 - 5.2

Install Add-on in Blender

Get the official Blender Extension installed on your PC or Mac:

  • 1Download the Blender Add-on (.zip) below. Do not extract the ZIP file.
  • 2Open Blender. In the top menu, go to Edit > Preferences > Get Extensions.
  • 3Click the arrow menu icon ⌄ in the top right corner and select Install from Disk...
  • 4Select the downloaded Alex1BC-Blender-Addon-0.1.0.zip file. Alex1BC enables automatically!
  • 5In your 3D Viewport, press N to open the sidebar, click the Alex1BC tab, and click Start Alex1BC. A QR code and pairing code will appear.
STEP 02 // PHONE ANDROID 8.0+

Install App on Your Phone

Get the controller ready on your handheld device:

  • 1Download Alex1BC-Android.apk directly to your phone (or install via adb install Alex1BC-Android.apk).
  • 2Open the downloaded APK on your phone and tap Install (allow install from your browser/files if prompted).
  • 3Launch Alex1BC and grant camera permission (needed to scan the pairing QR code).
  • ★Zero-Install Alternative: You can also simply scan the Blender QR code using your regular phone camera app to open the built-in Web Controller in Chrome or Safari without installing anything!
STEP 03 // SYNC 1-SECOND PAIR

Instant Pairing

Connect your phone to Blender with zero manual network setup:

  • 1Ensure your phone is connected to the same Wi-Fi network as your computer (or plugged in via USB cable).
  • 2In the Alex1BC phone app, tap Scan QR and point your camera at the QR code shown in Blender's 3D Viewport.
  • 3Connection established! The phone will vibrate with haptic confirmation and the live artificial horizon gyroscope will activate instantly.
  • 4Alternatively, you can manually type the 6-digit PIN displayed below the QR code.
STEP 04 // SHOOT TAKES & ANIMATION

Shoot & Animate

Perform natural handheld shots and bake them into editable keyframes:

  • 1Select Mode: Switch between GYRO (pure hand tilt), HYBRID (phone aims, sticks dolly & rise), or ANALOG (stick-only).
  • 2Recenter: Tap HOME anytime to align Blender's camera heading with your current phone orientation without jumps.
  • 3Record Takes: Tap REC to start live timeline playback and recording. Tap STOP when finished. A new Action (A1BC_Take_001) is created automatically!
  • 4Keyframing: Tap KEY to set an instant location, rotation, and focal length keyframe at the current timeline frame.
  • 5Safe & Non-Destructive: Existing camera animation is never erased. Click Undo last take in Blender to immediately restore previous animation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything You Need to Know

How does the phone communicate with Blender?
Alex1BC operates over your local network using secure WebSockets with TLS encryption. It runs entirely on your local machine-no telemetry, no cloud servers, and no internet required once downloaded. For zero-jitter physical studio shoots, you can also connect your phone via USB cable and use adb reverse.
Does recording takes overwrite my previous camera animation?
No! Alex1BC treats camera animation non-destructively. Every recording generates a brand new Blender Action (e.g. A1BC_Take_001, A1BC_Take_002). You can switch between takes in the Dope Sheet / NLA editor or tap Undo last take to immediately restore the camera's original animation.
Can I use the controller without installing the Android APK?
Yes. The Blender add-on includes a built-in mobile web controller served locally at https://<your-pc-ip>:47800. Simply scan the QR code using any phone's camera app to open the web controller instantly in Chrome, Safari, or Firefox.
What Blender versions are officially supported?
Alex1BC is engineered and tested against Blender 4.2 LTS, 5.0, 5.1.1, and 5.2 LTS. It strictly adheres to Blender's modern extension manifest specification (blender_manifest.toml).
Why is the motion so smooth without stuttering?
Unlike simple scripts that push blocking operators into Blender, Alex1BC uses a non-blocking queue and registers a native Blender window manager timer. Camera transformations are calculated smoothly on Blender's main thread loop at the viewport display rate.
